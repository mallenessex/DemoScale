Demand and supply

Now it sees the competition — and the newcomers

Where your buyers live is half the map. DemoScale filled in the rest: how crowded a market already is in your category, and the two audiences that aren't the settled locals.

Supply

How crowded is this market?

Establishment density from Census County Business Patterns, indexed against the national rate. Jackson Hole runs a food-and-drink index of 299 — triple the national saturation. A market thick with buyers and competitors is a different bet than one that's under-served.

Movers

Who just arrived?

Recent movers are high-intent — new address, new purchases. DemoScale reads IRS county-to-county migration to show where a market's newcomers came from and what they earn, kept separate from the settled residents.

Three audiences

Never summed by accident

Residents, movers, and visitors arrive as three distinct plans, each with its own source, vintage, and confidence. They overlap and are measured differently — so DemoScale refuses to add them into one "total market." No blended number you can't defend.

Why trust it

Every number arrives with its receipts

Smart-TV panels are enormous but biased. Vendors paper over the gap; DemoScale closes it with official statistics and tells you exactly how — every US total re-verifies against the Census's own national figures on demand, the same 37 live checks anyone can run. Four rules, enforced in the schema, not the marketing:

Rule one

Native grain or stop

US counties, UK local authorities, EU NUTS3 — loaded from census sources and reconciled against published totals before anything downstream may touch them. When a source doesn't support a claim, the system refuses the claim.

Rule two

Caveats are sentences

"England-only — Scottish deprivation isn't comparable." "This income screen is modeled, not enumerated." Limitations ship inside the answer, in plain speech — the only targeting source that tells you when not to use a number.

Rule three

Licensed assets stay yours

Nielsen market definitions, currency feeds, your sales data — bring-your-own, never bundled. Ask DemoScale for DMAs without a license and it declines, politely, with the reason. Procurement approves the tool that refuses correctly.

Rule four

A receipt on every number

Not just the headline — any figure in any answer. Ask where it came from and one call hands back its source, the exact table and field, the license, the vintage, and the caveats. Provenance isn't an appendix you hope exists; it's a number you can click.

How it works

From product to plan in one conversation

Who buys this?

Describe your product. Your AI gets spending indices by age and household income from federal expenditure data — who over-indexes, with sources — and a suggested starting audience.

Where do they live?

The audience is sized and ranked across every market in six countries — each in its own buying currency, with an index against the national average and the coverage stated.

Export for activation

The ranked table lands in your DSP's format, and the export writes a receipt. The conversation is the free demo; the export is the metered event.

And if you have your own panel DemoScale weights it: household resolution, geographic scaling to official universes, behavioral calibration to industry currency, and sales calibration that only ships if it survives a holdout test. Any stage missing its inputs refuses — and lists exactly what's needed.

The engine

Universes come from census sources; weighting is classical survey statistics — raking, rims, calibration — reproducible run over run and auditable line by line. The intelligence in the conversation is your model's; the truth in it is ours. That split is the design.
